A 3 nanometer chip doesn't have anything on it that's 3 nanometers. So what does the name mean?

Decades ago, the number matched a real feature on the chip, roughly the length of a transistor's gate.

As chips got smaller, that link broke. Today, the parts of a transistor are many times larger than the number in the name.

Now it's a generation label. Smaller numbers usually mean denser, more efficient chips. But one company's 3 nanometer isn't the same as another's. Intel even renamed one of its processes from 10 nanometer to Intel 7.

So compare real performance, not the number in the name. More PC hardware explained on the channel.
