Your Ryzen processor just hit 95 degrees. Is it about to cook itself?

No. AMD says recent Ryzen chips are built to run that hot. Under heavy load, a Ryzen 7000 chip pushes itself until it reaches its limit, about 95 degrees Celsius.

Heat is the brake. While there's thermal room left, the chip keeps raising its clock speed. At the limit, it holds steady instead of climbing higher.

AMD's guide for reviewers says these chips are designed to sit at that limit around the clock, without damage.

So a high number under load isn't a problem by itself. Worry if it runs hot while idle, or if performance suddenly drops. More PC hardware explained on the channel.
