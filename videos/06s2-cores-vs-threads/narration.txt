Your CPU might say 8 cores and 16 threads. So what's a thread, and why isn't it a core?

A thread is one stream of instructions. A core is the hardware that runs it: units that do the math, plus a record of where the thread is up to.

Cores often sit idle, waiting for data from memory. Hyper-Threading lets one core hold two threads. When one stalls, the other uses the idle units.

But both threads share the same units. That's why Intel's first version promised up to thirty percent more speed, not double.

Some chips skip it. Intel's Core Ultra 9 285K has 24 cores and just 24 threads, because Hyper-Threading was removed.
