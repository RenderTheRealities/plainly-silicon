DDR4 and DDR5 sticks both have 288 pins and the same length. So why won't one fit the other's slot?

Look at the gap in the bottom edge. That's the notch, and the slot has a matching ridge. On DDR5, it sits in a different spot.

It's a key, on purpose. The generations use different voltages and signals, so the wrong stick is blocked before it can do harm.

To check your board, look up its model and read the memory line on its spec page. CPU-Z shows the type you already have.

Shortcut: AMD's AM5 boards take only DDR5, AM4 only DDR4. Intel's twelfth to fourteenth gen boards come in both, so check.
