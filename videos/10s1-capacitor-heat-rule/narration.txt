In a cheap power supply, one of the first parts to wear out is a small can called an electrolytic capacitor.

These capacitors smooth the power your PC runs on. Each one is rated for a number of hours at a set temperature, like 5,000 hours at 105 degrees Celsius.

The rule of thumb is simple. Every 10 degrees cooler roughly doubles that life. At 95 degrees, about 10,000 hours. At 85, about 20,000.

It works the other way too. Run it hotter and its life shrinks just as fast.

That's why cheap units tend to die early. Smaller heatsinks and weaker fans let the capacitors run hotter, so they wear out sooner.

Keep yours cool with clean dust filters and room to breathe. The full story is in our video, Power Supplies Explained.
