## Through-line

One load screen, followed through every kind of drive: what the game asks for (thousands of scattered pieces), why a hard drive's arm turns that into a queue, why any SSD collapses the wait, why NVMe's huge sequential numbers mostly don't show up in load times (the CPU becomes the bottleneck), where SATA starts to fall behind (console-style streaming games, DirectStorage), and a final race. Visuals are green-phosphor terminal windows with log readouts, ASCII block bars, a seeking platter, flash chips read in parallel, PCIe lanes and a load pipeline.

## Title

SSD vs HDD vs NVMe: What Really Makes Games Load Faster

## Description

Same game, same PC: Cyberpunk 2077 loaded in 6 seconds from an SSD and 63 seconds from a hard drive, yet in many games a much faster NVMe drive saves a second or less. We follow one load screen through a hard drive, a SATA SSD and PCIe 3.0, 4.0 and 5.0 NVMe drives to show exactly where your waiting time goes, and when a faster drive is actually worth it.
