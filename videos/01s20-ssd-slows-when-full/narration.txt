Why does an S S D get slower as it fills up? It's not your imagination.

Many consumer drives use a trick. They run part of their empty flash in a fast mode that stores just one bit per cell. That's called an S L C cache, and new writes land there first.

The catch is that the cache is built from free space. As the drive fills, the cache shrinks. A big copy runs out of it sooner and drops to the slower native speed.

Free space also helps the controller tidy up, spreading out wear and clearing old blocks. With little room left, that cleanup slows your writes.

So leave some breathing room. A common rule of thumb is ten to twenty percent free.
