Your CPU can wait more than 350 cycles for one number from RAM. That's why it keeps tiny memories inside itself.

Take an AMD Zen four core. Closest of all is the level one data cache. It holds 32 kilobytes and answers in about 4 cycles.

Miss there, and the core checks level two. That's one megabyte, about 14 cycles away.

Next is level three, shared by the cores. It's roughly 50 cycles away.

From your RAM sticks, it takes about 73 nanoseconds. At 5 gigahertz, that's more than 350 cycles, roughly a hundred times slower than level one.

That's why big caches help games so much. The full trip is in our video, How a CPU Runs Your Code.
