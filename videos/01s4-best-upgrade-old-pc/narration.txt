If your old PC feels painfully slow, there's one upgrade that usually beats all the others.

Many older PCs still run on a hard drive. It stores data on spinning platters, read by an arm that has to physically move into place.

That's slow. A typical hard drive reads around 150 to 200 megabytes per second.

A solid state drive has no moving parts at all. Even a basic one is several times faster, and an NVMe SSD can read thousands of megabytes per second.

Boot times, app launches and loading screens all shrink. Before you buy anything else, check whether you're still on a hard drive. The full PC breakdown is on the channel.
