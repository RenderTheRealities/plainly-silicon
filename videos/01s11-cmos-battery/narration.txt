Your PC has a tiny battery inside it, even if it's a desktop that's always plugged in.

It's a coin cell, usually the same kind you'd find in a watch or a car key, sitting right on the motherboard.

Its job is to keep a small clock running when your PC is unplugged, and on many boards, to hold your BIOS settings.

It can last for years. But when it dies, the PC may forget the date and time every time it loses power, or reset its settings.

The fix is cheap. Replace the coin cell with the same type, with the power cable unplugged. More PC hardware explained on the channel.
