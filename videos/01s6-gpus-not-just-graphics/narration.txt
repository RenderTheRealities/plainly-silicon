Your graphics card has graphics in its name. But some of the most important work GPUs do today has nothing to do with games.

A GPU is thousands of small cores working at once. That's perfect for drawing pixels, but it's perfect for any job that splits into lots of small, independent pieces.

Artificial intelligence is the big one. Training and running AI models is mostly enormous piles of multiplication, and a GPU can do huge amounts of it in parallel.

Scientists use GPUs to simulate weather, molecules and proteins. Video editors use them to speed up effects and exports.

So a GPU isn't really a graphics chip. It's a parallel math machine that happens to be great at graphics. More PC hardware explained on the channel.
